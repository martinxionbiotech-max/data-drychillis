# Organic vs Conventional Paprika & Chilli

**TL;DR** — Organic certification controls how the crop is grown, not its heat or colour. Heat
(SHU) and colour (ASTA) depend on variety and ripening climate, so organic and conventional
paprika can be spec-identical in performance — the difference is sourcing, segregation and cost.
Choose organic only when your product carries an organic claim.

## What "Organic" Actually Changes

Organic certification governs agricultural inputs and process segregation:

| Aspect | Conventional | Organic |
|---|---|---|
| Pesticides / fertilisers | Synthetic inputs allowed | Certified organic inputs only |
| Growing base | Standard | Certified organic, segregated |
| Processing | Standard line | Segregated, audit-trailed |
| Certification | ISO / HACCP | Organic standard + ISO / HACCP |
| Cost | Baseline | Premium |

What it does **not** change is the fundamental specification. Heat comes from the variety's
capsaicin content; colour comes from carotenoid pigments. Both are set by variety and the
ripening-period climate — see [ASTA colour & SHU explained](../asta-shu-explained/index.md).

## When the Specifications Are Identical

A Xinjiang sweet pepper grown organically and one grown conventionally can carry the same ASTA
colour value and the same sub-500 SHU heat. The buyer who specifies "sweet paprika, 8–18 ASTA"
gets the same colouring performance either way. Organic status is a *process* claim, not a
*performance* claim.

## When Organic Matters

Choose organic paprika or chilli when:

- Your finished product carries an organic label or claim
- Your buyer or retailer requires certified organic ingredients
- Your target market (e.g. certain EU or US retail channels) demands organic certification

If none of these apply, conventional paprika meets the same specification at lower cost. The
decision is driven by your product's labelling, not by the chilli itself.

## The Cost and Availability Trade-off

Organic adds cost through:

- Certified, segregated growing bases
- Dedicated or segregated processing runs
- Certification audits and documentation

This is why organic paprika carries a premium, and why availability is more constrained than
conventional. Confirm availability and the specific organic standard (EU, USDA, etc.) with your
supplier before committing a product line to organic.

## What Buyers Should Ask

1. **Does my label require organic?** — the decision is driven by your product's labelling and
   target market, not by the chilli itself.
2. **Is the specification identical either way?** — if colour, heat and moisture are the same,
   the functional difference is documentation, not performance.
3. **What does organic cost me in MOQ and lead time?** — organic runs are smaller, so minimums
   and lead times shift.
