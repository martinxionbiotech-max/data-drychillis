# Shipping & Customs for Paprika and Chilli Imports

**TL;DR** — Ship bulk orders by sea, small or urgent orders by air. Prepare the customs document
set before the goods arrive — invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificates and COA — and
pick an Incoterm that matches how much shipping control you want. Dried chilli is easy to ship:
no cold chain, just moisture control.

## Sea Freight vs Air Freight

The first logistics decision is the transport method. It comes down to cost versus speed:

| Factor | Sea freight | Air freight |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per kg | Low — the standard for bulk | High — premium for speed |
| Transit time | Weeks | Days |
| Best for | Full-container bulk orders, regular supply | Samples, small orders, urgent restock |
| Minimum volume | Suits large runs | Economical only at low volumes |

For most food manufacturers, the pattern is clear: **sea freight for production supply, air
freight for samples and urgent gaps**. Dried paprika and chilli are dense, shelf-stable goods
that store well in a container, which makes sea freight the default for regular orders.

## The Customs Document Checklist

A complete import document set keeps customs clearance smooth. For dried paprika and chilli,
the typical set includes:

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Commercial invoice | Value and terms of the sale |
| Packing list | Quantities, weights and package breakdown |
| Bill of lading (or airway bill) | Proof of shipment and carrier |
| Certificate of origin | Confirms country of origin for tariff treatment |
| Health certificate | Food-safety attestation |
| Phytosanitary certificate | Plant-product attestation (chilli is a plant product) |
| Certificate of analysis (COA) | Batch-level colour, heat and moisture results |
| Specification sheet | The agreed variety, SHU, ASTA, mesh and moisture |

Requirements vary by destination country, so confirm the exact set with your supplier and
freight forwarder before the goods ship. For how the specification sheet and COA are built, see
[the buying guide](../buying-guide/index.md).

## Incoterms Explained

Incoterms define who pays for and controls each leg of the shipment. The most common for chilli
imports:

| Incoterm | What the seller handles | Buyer's role |
|---|---|---|
| **EXW** (Ex Works) | Goods at factory gate only | Buyer arranges everything downstream |
| **FOB** (Free On Board) | Goods loaded on the vessel | Buyer arranges freight + insurance |
| **CIF** (Cost, Insurance, Freight) | Freight and insurance to destination port | Buyer clears customs and takes delivery |
| **DAP / DDP** (Delivered) | Seller delivers to destination (DDP includes duty) | Buyer receives goods |

FOB and CIF are the workhorses for chilli imports. Choose FOB if you have a preferred forwarder
and want freight control; choose CIF if you want the seller to manage the ocean leg. For MOQ and
lead-time implications, see [importing & supply chain](../importing/index.md).

## Why Dried Chilli Is Easy to Ship

Dried paprika and chilli are among the simpler food products to import:

- **No cold chain** — they are shelf-stable at ambient temperature
- **Moisture control** — under 13% moisture keeps them stable through transit (see
  [colour change & storage](../colour-storage/index.md))
- **Sealed packaging** — protects against humidity during the ocean voyage

The one thing to get right is moisture. A dry, well-sealed product arrives in the same condition
it left the factory.
