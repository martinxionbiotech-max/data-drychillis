# Paprika & Chilli Quality Control

**TL;DR** — Chilli quality is three things — heat, colour and flavour — controlled through
variety, grade, impurity removal and moisture. The ripening-period climate is the biggest
single factor. A certified supplier with controlled growing bases delivers the consistency
batch after batch.

## The Three Quality Pillars

| Pillar | Measure | Controlled by |
|---|---|---|
| Heat | SHU (Scoville) | Variety + placenta content |
| Colour | ASTA value | Variety + processing |
| Flavour | Aroma profile | Variety + ripening climate |

These are independent. A chilli can be high-colour and mild (sweet paprika), or pale and
ferociously hot (small hot chillies). Quality control means holding all three to specification
simultaneously.

## The Control Levers

### 1. Variety + Grade

The single biggest lever. Locking the variety and grade determines the baseline heat, colour and
flavour. See [chilli varieties & grades](../chilli-varieties/index.md).

### 2. Impurity Control

Foreign objects and defects are removed physically:

- **De-seeding** — removes seeds (minor heat effect, major texture effect)
- **Magnet filtration** — removes ferrous metal
- **X-ray inspection** — detects dense foreign objects
- **Colour sorting** — removes off-colour and defective pieces by optical grading

### 3. Moisture Control

Moisture under 13% is standard for shelf stability, milling consistency and microbial safety.
Lower moisture plus cool, dry storage extends colour and flavour life — see
[paprika & chilli colour change and storage](../colour-storage/index.md).

### 4. Ripening-Period Climate

The climate during the late growth and ripening period is the single biggest factor in final
quality — it sets heat, colour and flavour at the source. This is why a supplier with multiple
controlled growing bases can smooth year-to-year variability better than one buying spot-market
raw material.

## The 8-Step Process

Raw material sourcing → pre-processing → grading → storage → processing → packaging → finished
goods — each step gated by a control point:

1. **Raw material sourcing** — approved suppliers, laboratory re-verification
2. **Pre-processing** — de-seeding, cleaning
3. **Grading** — size, colour, moisture classification
4. **Storage** — low-temperature warehouse
5. **Processing** — magnet filtration, metal detection
6. **Colour sorting & X-ray** — foreign-object removal, colour grading
7. **Packaging** — sealed, specification-labelled
8. **Finished goods** — final inspection before release

## How This Translates to Your Specification

Quality control is what makes a specification meaningful. A precise spec — variety, SHU, ASTA,
mesh, moisture — is only as good as the system behind it. Buying from a supplier running ISO
9001 and HACCP with batch documentation and raw-material traceability is how you turn a written
spec into a consistent product. See [importing & supply chain](../importing/index.md).
